# Proxies and index

Design notes behind `qcp -proxy` and `qcp -index`. Everything from "The library
as measured" down to "Design rules" is the reasoning that led to them; "Settled
by testing" and "As implemented" at the end record what shipped.

The problem is not playback performance. It is that the library is 98 hours
across 139 missions and 12 years, only the current year lives on a hot drive,
and there is no way to see what is in any of it without mounting a 22TB HDD and
opening clips one at a time.

The answer is a **browse tier** — 720p video plus poster frames and sprite
sheets, small enough to keep on the laptop forever — and a static index built
from it. An **edit tier** of ProRes proxies exists as an on-demand option, but
is deliberately not the default: measurement shows the camera originals already
play fine off T9, including four-layer mixed timelines. See "Why proxies — and
why *not* for editing" below.

Nothing here is archived; all of it is regenerable from the originals.

All numbers below were measured on 2026-08-24, on an M2 Pro (12 core, 32GB),
with ARCHIVE_01 and T9 mounted over USB. Anything estimated rather than
measured is marked as such.

---

## The library as measured

3,781 video files, 98.3 hours, 6.62TB, 2014–2026.

| Class                          | Files | Hours  | Size   | Avg bitrate |
| ------------------------------ | ----- | ------ | ------ | ----------- |
| XAVC-I 4K 4:2:2 10-bit (Sony)  | 2,530 | 37.9 h | 4.95TB | 290 Mbps    |
| HEVC 4K+ (GoPro 5.3K / DJI 4K) |   509 | 25.8 h | 1.09TB |  94 Mbps    |
| SD/HD misc (2014–2018 legacy)  |   657 | 33.6 h | 0.43TB |  28 Mbps    |
| ProRes                         |    35 |  0.4 h | 0.13TB | 748 Mbps    |

Growth is roughly 3TB/year: 2025 was 3.55TB, 2026 was 2.16TB by August.
ARCHIVE_01 has 15TiB free, so about five more years at that rate.

## Why proxies — and why *not* for editing

Editing the camera originals off T9 already works, and the measurements say it
should keep working. Concurrent decode, off T9, where a 24p timeline needs
24 fps per stream:

| Streams | XAVC-I per stream | GoPro HEVC (hw) per stream |
| ------- | ----------------- | -------------------------- |
| 1       | 232 fps (9.7×)    | 66 fps (2.75×)             |
| 2       | 116 fps (4.8×)    | 77 fps (3.21×)             |
| 3       |  78 fps (3.2×)    | 70 fps (2.92×)             |
| 4       |  58 fps (2.4×)    | —                          |

Mixed, 2× Sony plus 2× GoPro at once: Sony 4.17× and 4.08×, GoPro 2.58× and
2.71×. Every stream comfortably above realtime.

XAVC-I aggregate is a flat ~233 fps regardless of stream count — a fixed decode
budget of about 9.7 simultaneous 4K streams at 24p. Apple's decoder does not
handle H.264 High 4:2:2 Intra, so VideoToolbox is *worse* than software here
(3.1× vs 9.3× single-stream), but software is fast enough that it does not
matter.

GoPro HEVC looks alarming single-stream at 3.6× but it is dedicated silicon and
scales nearly linearly; per-stream throughput actually improves slightly at two
and three streams. Three 5.3K angles hold ~2.9× realtime each.

**So there is no edit-performance problem to solve, and the edit tier is not
worth a standing storage commitment.** An earlier draft of this document argued
for ~1TB of ProRes Proxy on T9 based on single-stream numbers; the concurrent
measurements above do not support it.

What remains true:

- **ARCHIVE_01 reads at 104 MB/s.** One XAVC-I stream is 36 MB/s, so under
  three concurrent streams saturates the drive, and that is sequential —
  scrubbing collapses it. Editing off the archive drive is not viable. But the
  workflow is to `-pull` a mission to T9 first, so this is mostly hypothetical.
- **The library is unbrowsable.** 98 hours across 139 missions and 12 years,
  with only the current year on a hot drive and no way to see what is in any of
  it. This is the actual problem, and it is not a playback problem.

The browse tier and index below address that. The edit tier is kept only as an
on-demand option for cases the measurements do not cover.

## Two tiers

### Edit tier — ProRes Proxy 1080p, on demand only

**Not generated by default.** The concurrent decode numbers above say the
originals play fine off T9, so this exists for cases they do not cover: a
render-heavy timeline with effects stacked on top of decode, working on battery
where pegging cores matters, or an unusually deep multicam.

Generated per mission when asked, not as a standing tier. Intra-frame so it
scrubs properly, and the M2 Pro has dedicated ProRes encode *and* decode
silicon.

If H.264 is used instead, force a short GOP (`-g 12`) or j-k-l scrubbing will
feel awful regardless of the throughput number.

Timecode survives ffmpeg untouched (verified: `04:20:30:12` on source and
proxy), which is what Resolve relinks on.

Note that the browse tier doubles as offline-edit media if editing away from the
drives is ever needed: the baked Rec.709 grade does not matter when the media
gets relinked to originals for the online, and timecode matches.

### Browse tier — H.264 720p 2.5 Mbps, plus stills

For the index. The whole library at 720p is ~111GB. The stills are much
smaller: a poster frame is 13KB and a 100-frame sprite sheet for hover-scrubbing
is 185KB, so **~750MB covers all 3,781 clips**.

That 750MB is the important number. It is small enough to live permanently on
the laptop, which means the index works with nothing plugged in. The 720p video
is optional and plays when a drive happens to be mounted.

Generate sprites **from the proxy, not the source**. Sprite generation off an
MXF took 44s for a 7-minute clip because it decodes everything; off the proxy it
is nearly free.

## Colour

This is the part with the traps in it.

### Do not bake the CST into the edit tier

If the proxy is graded and the camera original is not, toggling proxies in
Resolve makes the image jump and you are grading against a moving target. Set it
at the project level instead — Color Management, or a CST node on the timeline —
so proxy and original get identical treatment.

### Do bake it into the browse tier

A log thumbnail is a flat grey wash and tells you nothing about the shot. The
browse tier is never used for grading, so baking is correct there.

### The library needs three transforms, not one

Detection is free: it is in the `*M01.XML` sidecar next to every Sony clip, as
`CaptureGammaEquation` and `CaptureColorPrimaries`.

| Source                  | Clips | Treatment                     |
| ----------------------- | ----- | ----------------------------- |
| S-Log3 / S-Gamut3       | 1,963 | CST — no Sony LUT exists      |
| S-Log3 / S-Gamut3.Cine  |   470 | CST, or Sony's shipped LUT    |
| S-Cinetone / Rec.709    |    89 | **pass through untouched**    |
| GoPro / DJI             |   509 | already Rec.709, pass through |

The gamut changed mid-2026: all of 2025 is S-Gamut3, 2026 is mixed. Resolve only
ships `.Cine` LUTs, so 78% of the Sony footage has no vendor LUT at all.

Using the `.Cine` LUT on S-Gamut3 footage costs a median of 0 and a mean of
3.3/255, but p95 is 11/255 — negligible on neutral scenes, visible on saturated
subjects, which here means the wings. Detection is free, so do it properly.

2,523 of 2,530 MXF have a sidecar. The seven without need a fallback; inheriting
from the rest of the mission is reasonable, since capture settings do not change
mid-card.

### Trap 1: the range convention

Silent and easy to get backwards.

Sony's shipped LUTs are authored for Resolve's convention — legal 64–940
normalised to 0–1, so `in_range=limited`. A LUT computed against raw S-Log3 code
values needs `in_range=full`. Measured against a synthetic ramp of known code
values:

```
   CV  expected |  in_range=full  |  in_range=limited
  171    0.1540 |  0.1551 (+.001) |  0.0996 (-.054)
  300    0.3010 |  0.3011 (+.000) |  0.2693 (-.032)
  420    0.4894 |  0.4890 (-.000) |  0.4786 (-.011)
```

Backwards, you are off by up to 14/255 — enough to look wrong, subtle enough not
to notice.

### Trap 2: a technical CST clips this footage

Rec.709 holds about +2.47 stops over middle grey. On a representative flying
shot the *median* pixel is +3.31 stops and p95 is +5.25 — the camera is using
S-Log3's headroom exactly as intended. A naive CST blows the sky and the wing to
paper white.

A tone-map with a real shoulder is required. Extended Reinhard with the white
point at +5.5 stops works:

```
tonemap(x) = x * (1 + x/W²) / (1 + x)        W = 8.0
```

which maps 18% grey to 0.457 and rolls +5.5 stops into 1.0 without clipping.

### Validation

The computed transform and Sony's `SLog3SGamut3.CineToLC-709TypeA` on the same
frame agree to **mean 4.78/255 (1.9%), median 4**. Two independent derivations
landing in the same place is reasonable evidence both are right. The computed
one holds blacks at 3 where Sony's lifts to 53, which is expected — LC is a
low-contrast monitoring LUT.

Sanity checks that must hold: both gamut matrices preserve neutrals exactly
(rows sum to 1.0000), and S-Log3 CV 420 decodes to exactly 0.1800 linear.

33³ LUTs are sufficient at 970KB each. 65³ was tested and changed nothing
meaningful — the residual black lift of ~6/255 is inherent to gamma-encoding
steepness near zero, not grid resolution.

## Cost

Encode throughput and output size, measured:

| Pipeline                                        | Speed | Size at 98.3 h |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ----- | -------------- |
| XAVC-I → ProRes Proxy 1080p                     | 4.75× | 1.43TB         |
| XAVC-I → ProRes Proxy 720p                      | 6.25× | 738GB          |
| XAVC-I → H.264 1080p 12 Mbps                    | 5.84× | 531GB          |
| XAVC-I → H.264 720p 2.5 Mbps                    | 7.36× | 111GB          |
| GoPro HEVC → ProRes Proxy 1080p                 | 3.06× | —              |
| Dual output, one decode, no CST                 | 5.06× | —              |
| **Dual output, one decode, CST on browse leg**  | 3.20× | —              |

With the edit tier dropped from the default, the standing cost is the browse
tier plus stills: **~112GB for the whole 98-hour library**, against roughly
1.1TB had the ProRes tier been generated as originally planned.

Backfill is ~22 h of CPU against a **17.7 h I/O floor** (6.62TB at 104 MB/s), so
it stays I/O-bound either way — about a day of wall clock, or a year-folder at a
time. Sourcing from T9 where a mission exists there is 10× faster than
ARCHIVE_01.

The dual-output figure is retained because it still applies when the edit tier
*is* requested: one decode feeding both renditions costs 3.20× versus 4.19× for
the browse leg alone, so the second rendition is close to free — same instinct
as reading the source once and fanning out to N writers.

## Design rules

**Proxies are derived, not archived.** Never sync them to cold storage — cold
space is the scarce resource and they can always be regenerated. The 750MB
stills tier is the exception; it is cheap enough to keep everywhere.

**State stays on the drives.** Proxy trees mirror the mission layout and carry
their own manifest so the existing `-verify` and `-check` machinery works
unchanged. No database, same as everything else.

**Generate at ingest**, while the cards are still mounted, so backfill is a
one-time event rather than a permanent chore.

**Record the colour metadata in the index.** Storing the detected gamma and
gamut per clip makes "show me the S-Cinetone clips" a filter rather than a
rescan, and makes the transform auditable after the fact.

## Why this is not a performance problem

Worth recording, because the instinct to reach for a faster language here is
wrong and the measurements are unambiguous.

| | Throughput |
| --- | --- |
| BLAKE3 (Go, `lukechampine`, single-threaded) | 4.72 GB/s |
| SHA-256 stdlib, for scale | 2.59 GB/s |
| T9 read, uncached, no hashing | 996 MB/s |
| T9 read **+ BLAKE3**, uncached | 991 MB/s |
| ARCHIVE_01 read | 104 MB/s |

Reading and hashing costs the same as reading alone to within 0.5%. There is
4.7× headroom on the fast SSD and 45× on the archive HDD. A hash that ran in
zero time would save nothing.

`lukechampine` has no arm64 assembly and was expected to be a weak point. It is
not — 4.72 GB/s is competitive with optimised C.

The proxy pipeline has even less Go in it: the 3.20× figure is ffmpeg's decoder,
VideoToolbox's encode silicon, and `lut3d`. Across a full 3,781-file backfill,
process-spawn overhead is around three minutes against ~26 hours.

What would actually move the numbers, in order: a faster archive drive (104 MB/s
sets the 17.7 h floor and is 10× slower than T9), sourcing proxies from T9 where
the mission exists there, and the N-reads fix already scoped in the README.

## Settled by testing

Three things were open when this document was written. Two are now answered.

### Resolve proxy linking: mirrored proxy root, stem-preserving filenames

Blackmagic's own documentation gives the criteria an externally generated proxy
must meet to link in Resolve. There are four, and audio is not among them:

- identical timecode to the source
- **the same file name as the source, excluding extensions**
- the same frame rate as the source
- a format and codec Resolve supports

All four were checked against a generated browse proxy of `923_0322.MXF`:
timecode `07:15:49:00` on both source and proxy, frame rate 23.976 on both,
H.264/AAC in MP4, and the stem preserved. The ProRes edit tier clears the same
bar by construction.

That settles the layout question in favour of the **mirrored proxy root**, not
Blackmagic Proxy Generator's `.proxy` subfolder:

- BPG writes into a folder beside the source footage. That puts derived media
  inside the mission directory, where `checksums.b3` would cover it, `-sync`
  would carry it to cold storage and `-check` would count it as mission
  content. It buys automatic linking on import and costs the design rule that
  proxies are never archived. Not worth it.
- Resolve's own proxy generation location "organizes proxies into subfolders by
  original source clip location" — the same mirrored structure `qcp` writes —
  and the manual is explicit that proxy files "can then be moved to any other
  drive location on the system, and then re-linked to their source files".
- Linking a mirrored tree is one action, not one per clip: select the clips in
  the Media Pool, right-click → **Link Proxy Media**, and point the file browser
  at the directory. The manual calls out choosing a directory "in the case of
  multiple clips".

So proxies are named `<source stem>.mov` / `<source stem>.mp4` — the extension
changes, the stem never does — and the tree mirrors the mission layout under
`<drive>/proxies/<year>/<mission>/`. Relinking a mission means pointing Resolve
at `.../proxies/<year>/<mission>/edit/<card>/`.

The one thing still worth doing by hand is a first relink in the GUI, to
confirm nothing about this particular media surprises Resolve. The criteria are
met on paper and in the files.

### Audio channel mapping: one track is enough to link, but know what you lose

Sony clips do carry 8 separate mono PCM 24-bit streams — confirmed by ffprobe on
`923_0322.MXF`, which reports streams 1–8 as `pcm_s24le`, one channel each.

Resolve does not require them to be preserved: audio appears nowhere in the
four linking criteria above, so a proxy carrying only track 1 links exactly as
cleanly as one carrying all eight. **The chains are therefore unchanged — both
tiers still map `0:a:0`.**

What that costs is worth recording, because it is a workflow trap rather than a
linking one: while proxies are enabled, only CH1 is available. A cut that uses
an XLR mic on CH3/CH4 will play silent until proxies are turned off. If that
ever bites, the fix is one argument — map `0:a?` instead of `0:a:0` on the edit
tier, which preserves every stream and costs about 768 kbps of PCM against
ProRes Proxy 1080p's ~45 Mbps. It is deliberately not the default, because the
measured requirement does not call for it.

### Still open: missions without `checksums.b3`

**61 missions across 2017–2025 have no `checksums.b3`.** A proxy pass reads
every byte anyway, so it remains the natural moment to generate them in the same
sweep. `-proxy` does not do this yet: it takes the source BLAKE3 from
`checksums.b3` where one exists and falls back to size and mtime where one does
not, rather than paying for a second full read of the archive drive. Folding
`-checksum` into the same pass would remove that fallback entirely.

## As implemented

```
<drive>/proxies/
  luts/
    slog3_sgamut3_to_rec709.cube          generated at first use, 970KB each
    slog3_sgamut3cine_to_rec709.cube
  <year>/<NNN>_<name>/
    edit/<card_volume>/<clip>.mov         only when -tier edit|both
    browse/<card_volume>/<clip>.mp4
    stills/<card_volume>/<clip>.poster.jpg
    stills/<card_volume>/<clip>.sprite.jpg
    proxies.b3                            same format as checksums.b3
    proxies.json                          per-clip metadata and provenance
```

The proxy root sits at the drive root, outside the footage root, so nothing
that walks a year directory can see it. `-sync` and `-replicate` additionally
skip a directory named `proxies` by name, which is the guard that holds if the
layout ever moves.

`proxies.b3` is byte-identical in shape to `checksums.b3` — sorted
`blake3_hash  relative_path`, never describing itself — so the existing verify
machinery reads it unchanged. It covers `proxies.json` too.

`proxies.json` records per clip: source relative path, source BLAKE3 (from
`checksums.b3`), size and mtime, duration, resolution, frame rate, codec, the
detected `CaptureGammaEquation` and `CaptureColorPrimaries`, whether those came
from a sidecar or were inherited from the mission, and which transform was
applied. A re-run skips any clip whose recorded source hash still matches, and
reuses the cached sidecar reading rather than re-parsing every XML.

Renditions are written through a `.qcp-part` temporary and renamed on success,
so an interrupted run never leaves a truncated file that looks finished.

Concurrency is `min(source drive read limit, NumCPU/4)` ffmpeg processes — one
at a time off the archive HDD, three on the M2 Pro against a hot SSD.

`-index` reads the proxy manifests and the `checksums.b3` files across every
mounted drive and emits `index.html`, `index.json` and a copy of the stills
tree. The data is inlined into the page as well as written to `index.json`,
because a `file://` page cannot fetch a sibling JSON file — every browser
treats that as a cross-origin request, and the whole point of the index is that
it opens with nothing mounted.

### Verified against the measurements

The generated S-Gamut3.Cine cube is 970,516 bytes, matching the 970KB in the
cost table. Both gamut matrices have rows summing to 1.000000000. S-Log3 CV 420
decodes to exactly 0.180000 linear and CV 598 to 0.900840. The tone-map puts 18%
grey at 0.457356 after the OETF and +5.5 stops at exactly 1.0.

Running the generated cube through ffmpeg's `lut3d` on a synthetic neutral ramp
reproduces the range trap exactly as recorded above:

```
   CV  |  in_range=full  |  in_range=limited  |  Go
  171  |  0.1544         |  0.1007            |  0.1533
  300  |  0.2942         |  0.2649            |  0.2943
  420  |  0.4575         |  0.4497            |  0.4574
  598  |  0.7361         |  0.7703            |  0.7369
```

Full range tracks the Go implementation within 0.0012; limited range is off by
up to 0.053, or 13/255. CV 95 reads 0.0235 rather than 0 — the lift is inherent
to gamma steepness near zero, not a bug, and the tests assert it stays under
0.04.

(The `expected` column in the range-trap table further up was measured against a
plain gamma LUT with no shoulder, which is why its CV 420 figure is 0.4894 —
`0.18 ^ (1/2.4)` — rather than the 0.4574 the shipped transform produces. Both
are correct; they are different LUTs, and only the second bakes a tone-map.)

### A known cost

`scale=1280:-2` and `scale=1920:-2` are absolute, so the 657 legacy SD/HD clips
from 2014–2018 are upscaled rather than left alone. It is wasteful but small —
that class is 0.43TB of the 6.62TB library and encodes very fast — and it keeps
the filter chains identical to the ones the cost table was measured with.
